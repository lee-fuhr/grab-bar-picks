How to choose grab bars

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Choose grab bars made of stainless steel for durability and rust resistance, ensure they are rated for at least 250 pounds, and match the mounting type (wall studs, anchors, or direct-to-stud) to your bathroom wall construction. Prioritize textured or knurled surfaces for wet-hand grip, and pick a length that fits the specific spot, usually 12 to 36 inches, where you need support near the toilet, shower, or tub.

What is the best material for bathroom grab bars?

Stainless steel is the gold standard because it won’t rust or corrode in a humid bathroom, and it’s strong enough to handle serious weight without bending. Many stainless steel bars have a brushed or satin finish that hides water spots and fingerprints better than polished chrome. Some bars are made of nylon-coated steel or plastic, but those can crack over time or feel less secure under a heavy load. Stick with type 304 or 316 stainless steel, both are corrosion-resistant, with 316 offering extra protection if you live near saltwater coastal air.

How much weight should a grab bar support?

Look for grab bars with a published weight capacity of at least 250 pounds, though many heavy-duty models are tested to 500 or more. The number matters because a person may grab the bar suddenly or lean their full body weight during a fall, and the bar needs to hold without pulling out of the wall. Pay attention to the fine print: some bars are rated only when installed into wood studs, not into drywall anchors. Never assume a bar is strong enough just because it feels solid, always check the manufacturer’s load rating and install according to their instructions.

What mounting type do I need: stud, anchor, or surface-mount?

The safest installation is directly into wood wall studs with corrosion-resistant screws, because studs provide the strongest anchor point. If you cannot find studs in the ideal location, common behind tile or shower surrounds, you can use heavy-duty toggle bolts or specialized snap-toggle anchors designed for grab bars. Avoid plastic expansion anchors; they are not reliable under sudden load. Surface-mount bars that attach to the wall with a backing plate are fine if the plate is large enough and the wall is solid (e.g., concrete, brick, or tile over cement board). For a tub transfer bench or beside the toilet, consider bars that come with a flange that hides the hardware for a cleaner look.

What length and placement work best for common bathroom spots?

Next to the toilet, a 24- or 36-inch bar mounted horizontally or at a slight angle (about 15 degrees from horizontal) helps you stand and sit. In a shower or tub, a 16- to 24-inch vertical bar near the control valve is excellent for balance while turning, and a 24-inch horizontal bar on the back wall gives leverage for washing legs or stepping out. For a walk-in shower, a longer bar, up to 48 inches, along the back wall can double as a safety rail and a towel rack. Always simulate the motion: have the person who will use it most stand or sit in the spot and reach out naturally to see where their hand lands.

Should I look for ADA-compliant grab bars even if I don’t need certification?

Yes, because ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act) guidelines represent best practices for grip, placement, and strength. ADA-compliant bars have a diameter between 1.25 and 1.5 inches, which is comfortable for most hands and easy to wrap fingers around. They also require a specific distance from the wall (typically 1.5 inches) so a person can slide their hand all the way around without pinching. Even if you aren’t building to code, following those dimensions means the bar will be safer and easier to use for anyone with limited hand strength or arthritis.

Does the grip surface matter for wet hands?

Absolutely. A smooth chrome bar can feel dangerously slippery when your hand is wet or soapy. Look for bars with a textured or knurled surface, or a powder-coated finish that adds friction. Stainless steel bars with a diamond or cross-hatch pattern are common and very effective. Some bars come with a rubberized grip sleeve, but make sure the sleeve is removable for cleaning, mold and soap scum can hide underneath. If you choose a smooth bar, pair it with a hand lotion or a non-slip grip tape (stick-on) as a workaround, but a built-in texture is far more reliable.

Frequently asked questions

Can I install a grab bar without finding a stud?

Yes, but only if you use heavy-duty toggle bolts or snap-toggles rated for the bar’s weight capacity, do not rely on plastic drywall anchors. For tile or fiberglass surrounds, you may need to cut a hole and install a blocking plate first.

What is the best diameter for a grab bar?

1.25 to 1.5 inches in diameter is the sweet spot. That range fits most adult hands and gives enough surface to grip firmly without being too thick to close your fingers around.

Are suction-cup grab bars safe for showers?

No. Suction-cup bars can lose adhesion suddenly, especially on textured tile or in hot, steamy conditions. They are not a substitute for permanently mounted grab bars. Use them only as temporary aids, never as primary fall protection.

How do I know if my wall can support a grab bar?

Look for wood studs using a stud finder, or check for solid backing behind tile by tapping for a hollow sound vs. a solid thud. If you have metal studs, you need special toggle anchors designed for them. If in doubt, consult a contractor or handyman who has experience with bathroom grab bar installation.

Can I reuse an existing towel bar as a grab bar?

No. Standard towel bars are not load-rated and their mounting brackets are often weak plastic or thin metal that can pull out with less than 50 pounds of force. They are a serious fall risk if used for support.

Do I need one grab bar or multiple?

That depends on the user’s mobility and bathroom layout. At minimum, install one bar by the toilet and one in the shower or tub area. If someone uses a walker or wheelchair, add a vertical bar near the doorway and a horizontal bar at the sink for balance while standing.

In shortChoosing the right grab bar is about matching the material, mounting, length, and grip to the actual needs of the person using it and the construction of your bathroom. Stainless steel bars installed into studs with a textured surface and a 1.25- to 1.5-inch diameter will serve you for decades. Take the time to measure, check the wall backing, and install it securely, a well-chosen grab bar is one of the smartest investments you can make for everyday safety.
